    The model

    Outsource production. Outsource fulfilment.

    You have ten hours a week and under a lakh. That budget cannot buy inventory, acquisition and your own time back. So buy back the time by removing yourself from two whole functions.

    FunctionBeforeNowWhy

    Storing and shippingSelf-ship, ₹52/orderAmazon FBANo packing, no couriers, no returns handling. Prime badge. The only model that survives 10 hrs/week.
    Finding customers₹2,700 of ads, onceAmazon search first, then paidAmazon already has buyers typing "hazelnut instant coffee". You rank rather than buy them all.

    Why FBA is the whole answer to your 2023 problem

    • The warehouses are already on your GST. Bhiwandi and Chakan. Done.
    • Referral fee is currently 0% for Grocery & Gourmet priced ₹300–1,000 — a policy Amazon introduced in March 2026. Below ₹1,000 you pay a closing fee and weight handling, and no commission at all.
    • FBA closing fees are lower than self-ship, and FBA weight handling on a small parcel beats the ₹52 you were paying.
    • Prime badge. This is the part people underestimate — it lifts both conversion and search placement, and you cannot get it self-shipping.
    • Zero touch per order. Ten orders a day costs you the same time as zero.
    One pricing constraint, and it's structural

    Keep every listing priced between ₹300 and ₹1,000. Below ₹300 the closing fee eats a small order alive; above ₹1,000 a 10% referral fee kicks in. That band is where a 0% commission currently lives, and it should shape your pack sizes rather than the other way round. Treat 0% as a window that may close — sanity-check that the business still works at 7%.

    MOQ is per flavour, not per order

    Every listing found prices and minimums per SKU — each flavour, format and size is its own product line. Four flavours means four minimum orders. At the low end that might be four small runs you can afford; at the sachet end, minimums of 2,000–35,000 pieces per flavour will blow the budget instantly. No supplier publishes a combined-across-flavours minimum. It can sometimes be negotiated, but never assume it.

    So launch two flavours, not four

    Take your two strongest sellers and put the full budget behind them properly, rather than spreading it across four half-funded SKUs. Add the others from revenue once the first batch sells through. A four-flavour range with none of them in stock is worse than two flavours always available — and on Amazon, going out of stock costs you search ranking that takes weeks to rebuild.

    Pair them as a duo pack as well as singles. That gives you a higher-priced listing inside the ₹300–1,000 band without needing a third flavour, and it is a natural gift format.

    A detail that may cut your cost

    One supplier's finished, private-labelled jar works out cheaper per kilo than several suppliers' raw bulk coffee prices. That's a single data point from listing prices and may not survive contact with real quotes — but it means the assumption that private label carries a big premium over repacking yourself is worth testing rather than accepting. Ask every supplier for two quotes: finished-and-labelled, and bulk unbranded. Compare them properly before deciding.

    Compliance · this changed

    You become a Relabeller — and a deemed manufacturer

    Correction to earlier advice

    I previously told you the category was Marketer. That is wrong: FSSAI discontinued the Marketer Kind of Business around September 2021 and stopped accepting new applications under it. The correct KOB for a brand owner whose product is made by a licensed third party is Relabeller.

    Your current licence says Repacker — General Manufacturing. That described what you used to do. It does not describe private labelling. When you go to FoSCoS you are doing three things at once, not one:

    1. Renewing the licence that expired 23 June 2024
    2. Changing or adding the KOB to Relabeller
    3. Correcting the premises address — the licence says Keshavnagar Mundhwa 411036, your invoices say Hinjewadi 411057
    You probably need two Kinds of Business

    Relabeller to own the brand on product made by someone else, and Retailer because Amazon requires a Retailer KOB on the FSSAI licence for FBA eligibility. Raise both in the same application rather than discovering the second one when your FBA shipment gets blocked. Whether a single licence can carry both simultaneously was not something I could confirm from an authoritative source — ask FoSCoS directly.

    What "deemed manufacturer" means for you

    This is the part that genuinely matters and that most brand owners discover too late. As a Relabeller you are legally treated as the manufacturer of the product. Liability follows your brand name, not the factory gate. In practice:

    • You may contract only with an FSSAI-licensed manufacturer, and you must hold a copy of their valid licence covering the correct product category
    • You are responsible for Schedule 4 hygiene compliance at their facility — which is why visiting or auditing is not optional theatre
    • You must maintain a recall and traceability plan, and be able to trace any unit back to a batch
    • You must still conduct product testing — outsourcing production does not outsource this
    • You must file the annual return each year
    • Both FSSAI numbers go on the label: Manufactured by [maker, address, their licence no.] and Marketed by Buzzcaf Private Limited, [address], 11522079000056

    The upside is real, though: no premises to bring to manufacturing standard, no equipment, no packing labour, and someone else's quality systems doing the work that failed last time.

    Timing

    Diwali is reachable. That doesn't mean chase it.

    Correction to earlier advice

    I said private label lead times run 4–8 weeks and Diwali probably wouldn't fit. Indian instant coffee suppliers are quoting roughly 1–3 weeks. The window is open.

    Diwali falls in early November 2026. Amazon gifting traffic peaks through late October, and FBA stock must be shipped, received and receipted before that — so inventory needs to leave the manufacturer by early October. Working back through a 2–3 week production run, you'd need a supplier chosen and artwork finalised within about two weeks of today.

    That's possible. It is also exactly how you get a repeat of 2023.

    Chase DiwaliTarget January Recommended

    Shelf-life testingSkippedFour-week test completed before committing

    The middle path, and what I'd actually do: run proper supplier diligence on the January timeline, but if a supplier can turn a genuinely small first run quickly, put a modest quantity live for Diwali as a market test. You learn whether the listing converts, on real traffic, without betting the budget on an unvetted batch. Your history says rushing production is precisely what broke this business — don't repeat it for one season.

    Sequence · ten hours a week

    The next 90 days

    Week 1

    Unblock

    • Check both domain registrars — buzzcaf.in and buzzcaf.com. Recover or re-register today.
    • Log into Amazon Seller Central. Confirm account health, category approval, and what listings survived.
    • Log into FoSCoS. Renew, change KOB to Relabeller, add Retailer, fix the address. Ask explicitly whether both KOBs can sit on one licence.
    • Email all six suppliers. Ask for price per kg and per finished unit, MOQ per flavour, whether MOQ can be split across flavours, lead time, paid samples, FSSAI licence copy, and a spec sheet stating whether flavourings are natural or artificial.
    Weeks 2–5

    Choose the manufacturer

    • Get paid samples from at least three. Taste blind, black and with milk, at the dose a customer would use.
    • Run the four-week test on the front-runner: some sealed, some in heat and humidity, some opened daily. Instant coffee cakes when it takes on moisture — this is the failure mode that reads to a customer as a cheap product.
    • Verify FSSAI licences. Ask for recent test reports. Visit the front-runner if it's reachable — you carry their liability.
    • Pick two flavours. Get final artwork done with both FSSAI numbers and the full Legal Metrology declarations.
    Weeks 4–7

    Build the listings while production runs

    • Place the first order. Insist on a pre-production sample against your artwork before the full run.
    • Enrol in Brand Registry on your registered trademark.
    • Build the listings properly: seven images, A+ content, titles written for search. This is the highest-leverage work in the whole plan and it costs nothing but hours.
    • Put up the five-page website. One weekend, then leave it alone.
    Weeks 7–10

    Go live

    • Inbound to FBA. Allow real time for receipting — it is slower than you expect and it is not in your control.
    • Restart Instagram on the existing handle. Post the process; you shoot for a living.
    • Use "Request a Review" on every single order. Never incentivise reviews — it risks the account you just recovered.
    • No ad spend yet.
    Weeks 10–13

    Only now, advertise

    • Once you hold a genuine review base, start a small Sponsored Products budget on your brand and close category terms.
    • Watch sell-through against stock. Reorder before you run out — going out of stock costs ranking that takes weeks to recover.
    • Add flavours three and four from revenue, not from the original budget.
    • Review honestly against the criteria below.

    The gap you have to accept

    There is no meaningful advertising money in this budget, because inventory consumes it. That is the real cost of choosing B2C over B2B: you must earn your first customers from Amazon search, reviews and organic reach rather than buying them. It works — the 0% referral fee and the Prime badge are genuinely favourable right now — but it is slower, and it means the quality of your listing matters more than anything else you will do. Treat those seven images and that A+ content as the most important work of the quarter, because they are.
